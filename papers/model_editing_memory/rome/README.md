# Locating and Editing Factual Associations in GPT
arXiv: 2202.05262 | Kevin Meng et al. (MIT CSAIL / Northeastern University / Technion, 2022)
Code: https://rome.baulab.info/

## Files

- `s01_abstract_intro.md` — Frames the paper’s central claim that factual associations in autoregressive transformers correspond to localized, directly editable computations. It introduces the two-part strategy: use causal tracing to identify decisive hidden states, then test the resulting hypothesis by editing one MLP layer with Rank-One Model Editing (ROME). The target behavior is not mere answer memorization, but an edit that generalizes across prompts while remaining specific to the subject.
- `s02_causal_tracing.md` — Develops the clean/corrupted/restored intervention used to measure the indirect causal effect of each hidden state. On 1,000 known facts in `GPT-2 XL`, it finds an early site at the last subject token where mid-layer MLPs dominate, and a late site where attention moves recalled information to the prediction position. Path-specific interventions support the hypothesis that mid-layer MLP computations perform factual recall.
- `s03_rome_method.md` — Treats an MLP projection matrix as a linear associative memory and derives ROME’s constrained rank-one update. It details subject-key extraction, target-value optimization with a KL regularizer, and the closed-form insertion $\hat W=W+\Lambda(C^{-1}k_*)^T$. The method changes one fact in one layer without training an auxiliary editor.
- `s04_evaluation.md` — Covers `zsRE`, the new 21,919-record `COUNTERFACT` benchmark, layer/token localization experiments, generation comparisons, and human evaluation. ROME reaches **89.2** editing score on `GPT-2 XL` and **91.5** on `GPT-J`, combining near-perfect efficacy and paraphrase generalization with much stronger neighborhood specificity than fine-tuning or hypernetwork baselines.
- `s05_related_conclusion_ethics.md` — Connects ROME to probing, causal mediation, language-model knowledge extraction, Knowledge Neurons, KE, and MEND. It records the core limitations—single, directional factual edits and no treatment of logical, spatial, or numerical knowledge—and the ethical risk that direct editing can insert misinformation as easily as it can correct errors.
- `s06_references.md` — Full 43-entry reference list covering associative memories, causal analysis, factual probing, transformer interpretability, and model editing.
- `s07_appendix_rank_one_derivation.md` — Preserves the equality-constrained least-squares derivation behind ROME. Starting from the normal equations, it shows why the minimum-interference update is rank one and derives the exact residual vector $\Lambda$ needed to satisfy the new key–value constraint.
- `s08_appendix_causal_tracing.md` — Gives the causal-tracing experimental settings, cross-model results, corruption-rule checks, counterexamples, and the comparison with integrated gradients. The early-site MLP pattern persists from `GPT-2 Medium` through `GPT-NeoX-20B`, although the decisive subject token and peak layer can vary.
- `s09_appendix_datasets_implementation.md` — Documents `zsRE` and `COUNTERFACT` construction, all baseline implementations, ROME hyperparameters, and extended smaller-model results. ROME uses 100,000 Wikipedia-derived key samples, edits layer 18, optimizes the value for at most 20 steps, and takes about **2 seconds** per `GPT-2 XL` edit on an A6000.
- `s10_appendix_qualitative_attention_human.md` — Collects generation examples, dataset records, the late-layer attention-editing ablation, and human-study details. Attention editing can regurgitate the target under the original prompt but fails to generalize, supporting the distinction between MLP-based factual recall and attention-based word prediction; 15 evaluators also preferred ROME’s factual consistency over `FT+L`.
