# LIMA: Less Is More for Alignment
arXiv: 2305.11206 | Zhou et al. (Meta AI / Carnegie Mellon University / USC / Tel Aviv University, 2023)
Code: — (dataset: https://huggingface.co/datasets/GAIR/lima)

## Files

- `s01_introduction.md` — Abstract and Section 1. Introduces the **Superficial Alignment Hypothesis**: most knowledge and capabilities are acquired during pretraining, while alignment mainly teaches the model the interaction style/format needed to expose them. The paper tests this by fine-tuning a `65B` `LLaMa` model on only **1,000** carefully curated prompt-response examples, with no RLHF or preference modeling, and reports that LIMA is equal-or-preferred to `GPT-4` in **43%** of pairwise human comparisons.
- `s02_alignment_data.md` — Section 2. Details the 1,000-example alignment dataset: 400 `Stack Exchange` examples, 200 `wikiHow` articles, 150 `r/WritingPrompts` examples, 200 author-written assistant-style responses, and 50 `Super-Natural Instructions` examples. It emphasizes input diversity plus stylistically consistent high-quality outputs, with a 50-example dev set and 300 test prompts.
- `s03_training_lima.md` — Section 3. Records the exact training recipe: supervised fine-tuning from `LLaMa-65B`, an end-of-turn token separating user/assistant utterances, 15 epochs of `AdamW`, learning rate decay from `$10^{-5}$` to `$10^{-6}$`, batch size 32, 2048-token truncation, and residual dropout increasing to `$p_d=0.3$`. It also notes the practical finding that validation perplexity did not track generation quality.
- `s04_human_evaluation.md` — Section 4. Covers pairwise human and `GPT-4` preference evaluation against `Alpaca-65B`, `DaVinci003`, `Bard`, `Claude`, and `GPT-4`; generation settings; annotation protocol; and inter-annotator agreement. Main results: LIMA outperforms `DaVinci003` and `Alpaca-65B`, is equal-or-preferred to `Bard` in **58%**, to `Claude` in **46%**, and to `GPT-4` in **43%** of cases; absolute analysis labels **50%** of LIMA outputs excellent and **88%** as meeting prompt requirements.
- `s05_ablations.md` — Section 5. Presents ablations on data diversity, response quality, and quantity using `LLaMa-7B` models scored by `ChatGPT` on a 1–6 helpfulness scale. Diverse, filtered `Stack Exchange` data beats homogeneous `wikiHow` data; filtered data beats unfiltered data by about **0.5** Likert points; simply increasing `Stack Exchange` quantity by up to **16×** plateaus, supporting the claim that quality and diversity matter more than raw example count.
- `s06_dialogue_discussion.md` — Sections 6–7. Tests whether a model trained only on single-turn examples can conduct multi-turn dialogue, then shows that adding just **30** dialogue chains raises excellent dialogue turns from **45.2%** to **76.1%** and drops failures from **15/42** turns to **1/46**. The discussion frames LIMA as evidence for the strength of pretraining while acknowledging that manual data curation is hard to scale and LIMA is less robust than product-grade assistants.
- `s07_references.md` — Full 26-entry reference list covering instruction tuning, RLHF, Constitutional AI, `LLaMa`, `Alpaca`, `Self-Instruct`, `Super-Natural Instructions`, sampling/decoding, and prior work on few-shot alignment.
- `s08_appendices.md` — Appendices A–E. Records the training-example gallery, the anti-correlation between validation perplexity and generation quality, the human annotation interface, the `ChatGPT` scoring rubric, and the format-constraint experiment where six structure-oriented examples enable LIMA to produce complex structured outputs such as a marketing plan.
