# Jailbreaking Leading Safety-Aligned LLMs with Simple Adaptive Attacks
arXiv: 2404.02151 | Maksym Andriushchenko et al. (EPFL, 2024)
Code: https://github.com/tml-epfl/llm-adaptive-attacks

## Files

- `s01_frontmatter_introduction.md` — Covers the abstract, motivation, contributions, and headline Table 1. The paper argues that leading safety-aligned LLMs remain brittle under simple **adaptive attacks** tailored to each model family. Its headline result is **100% attack success rate** on a broad set of open-weight and proprietary models under the authors' GPT-4 semantic judge, with caveats about false positives on Claude 2.1.
- `s02_related_work.md` — Situates the work among manual jailbreaks, direct search attacks, and LLM-assisted red-teaming methods. The section distinguishes handcrafted templates, gradient or zeroth-order suffix search, and auxiliary-LLM methods such as PAIR, TAP, persona modulation, and GPTFUZZER. It also explains why earlier OpenAI logit-bias-based attacks were no longer directly applicable.
- `s03_background_methodology.md` — Defines the threat model, judge-based success criterion, and the authors' adaptive-attack framing. It details the paper's building blocks: prompt templates, random search over adversarial suffixes, **self-transfer**, cross-model transfer, and Claude-style prefilling. Verbatim jailbreak prompt templates are intentionally not reproduced here; the file preserves their role and experimental meaning without operational prompt text.
- `s04_jailbreaking_results.md` — Transcribes the main results for `Llama-2/3`, `Gemma`, `R2D2`, GPT, and Claude models. The central empirical point is that no single static attack works across all targets: Llama-family models need self-transfer, `R2D2` is most vulnerable to an in-context prompt variant, GPT models exploit logprob access, and Claude models are most affected by transfer or prefilling.
- `s05_trojan_detection.md` — Describes the related SaTML 2024 universal trojan-string detection task. The authors adapt random search by restricting candidate tokens based on embedding-distance heuristics, yielding the winning score among competition submissions and showing the same adaptivity principle outside direct jailbreak evaluation.
- `s06_discussion_limitations.md` — Summarizes the paper's methodological takeaways, recommendations, and limitations. The authors emphasize adaptive evaluation, note that API design can both help and hinder attacks, and caution that a perfect jailbreak-judge score does not always imply practically useful harmful content.
- `s07_references.md` — Full 62-entry reference list with compact author names, titles, venues/sources, and corrected years where the HTML parser produced ambiguous values.
- `s08_appendix_additional_discussions.md` — Covers the ethics statement, the formal definition of adaptive attacks, and ICLR-discussion Q&A. The appendix clarifies that adaptivity is defined relative to a defense, not merely a model, and argues that fixed attack suites alone cannot establish robustness without provable guarantees.
- `s09_appendix_experimental_details.md` — Records experimental details for random search, system prompts, and trojan detection. It keeps the algorithmic setup, iteration counts, suffix length, restart policy, and model-specific prompt/system-message usage while omitting verbatim jailbreak templates that would be operational attack strings.
- `s10_appendix_additional_results.md` — Collects ablations and additional tables: suffix-length effects, runtime, Claude transfer and prefilling ablations, judge comparisons, direct baseline comparisons, and the full model-evaluation summary. Verbatim harmful generations in Appendix C.4 are summarized and explicitly marked as omitted.
