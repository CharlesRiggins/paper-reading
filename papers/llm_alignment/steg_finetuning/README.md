# Invisible Safety Threat: Malicious Finetuning for LLM via Steganography

arXiv: 2603.08104 | Guangnian Wan, Xinyin Ma, Gongfan Fang, Xinchao Wang et al. (National University of Singapore, 2025)
Code: https://github.com/bigglesworthnotacat/LLM-Steg

## Files

- `s01_introduction.md` — Front matter (title, authors, abstract) and Section 1. Introduces the core threat: a finetuned LLM can maintain a facade of safety alignment while covertly generating harmful content via invisible-character (zero-width) steganography. The finetuning dataset contains no plaintext harmful content (evading data filters), and the stegotext outputs evade both human reviewers and automated guards like Llama Guard. Contributions: a steganographic finetuning method, exposure of a blind spot in current safety mechanisms, and validation on GPT-4.1, Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct, Phi-4, and Mistral-24B-Base.
- `s02_method.md` — Section 2. Defines two threat models (API-only finetuning of closed models vs. full control of open-source models) and the defense mechanism (training-data moderation + inference-time content moderation). Details invisible character steganography (five zero-width Unicode characters parsed by tokenizers but invisible in chat UIs) and the two-track multitask finetuning scheme pairing the target steganographic encoding with a structure-aligned auxiliary base-4 encoding, plus four process-supervision subtasks. ~99% of data is benign; malicious payloads come from STAR-1 prompts jailbroken via Qwen-2.5-32B (350 samples for GPT-4.1, 850 per open-source model).
- `s03_experiments.md` — Section 3. Safety evaluation on AdvBench (520 instructions) with Llama-Guard-3-8B: 100% of stegotext pairs classified safe before decoding, >90% unsafe after decoding (GPT-4.1 93.3%, Llama-3.3-70B 96.4%, Phi-4 94.6%, Mistral-24B-Base 95.0%), with refusal rates on plaintext harmful prompts preserved or even improved. Utility evaluation across five benchmarks shows only modest degradation (e.g., ~5 pts on MMLU, GPQA drop from 66.3→48.7 vs. benign-FT 55.2). Proposes two defenses: character filtering and frequency-penalty generation.
- `s04_05_06_related_work_limitations_conclusion.md` — Sections 4–6 plus ethics and acknowledgement. Related work covers malicious finetuning and steganography with LLMs (notably Halawi et al. 2024). Limitations: some prompts yield benign/decoded-off-target outputs and the encoding inflates token counts. Conclusion: the method bypasses OpenAI's finetuning-API safeguards and evades Llama Guard, exposing a blind spot in current safety mechanisms. The attack was disclosed to OpenAI; no human subjects involved.
- `s08_references.md` — Full 61-entry reference list spanning jailbreak research (Zou et al., Wei et al., Anil et al.), malicious finetuning (Yang, Zhan, Yi, Halawi), steganography (Petitcolas, Kaushik & Bhardwaj), model releases (GPT-4.1, Llama 3, Phi-4, Qwen2.5, Mistral), and evaluation benchmarks (MMLU, GPQA, MGSM, HumanEval, SimpleQA, HellaSwag, PIQA, WinoGrande, AdvBench, JailbreakBench).
- `s09_appendix_a_training_examples.md` — Appendix A. Concrete training examples for the steganographic encoding (system/user/assistant triples) and the auxiliary base-4 encoding, including subtasks 1–4 for both schemes and a decoded malicious sample showing how harmful content is embedded only in stegotext (the training set never contains decoded malicious text).
- `s10_appendix_b_sample_transcripts.md` — Appendix B. Nine decoded successful attack transcripts from the GPT-4.1 AdvBench evaluation (cover inputs/outputs are benign; target outputs are harmful), plus a failure case where a generic cover prompt yields off-target output, fixed by a cover prompt customized to the target topic. Demonstrates the model's dual behavior: refusing plaintext malicious queries while answering the hidden steganographic version.
- `s11_appendix_c_implementation_details.md` — Appendix C. Hyperparameters/hardware notes (OpenAI finetuning API for GPT-4.1; LoRA for open-source models) and the five utility-evaluation datasets from OpenAI's simple-evals (MMLU, GPQA-Diamond, MGSM, HumanEval, SimpleQA).
- `s12_appendix_d_more_experimental_results.md` — Appendix D. Extended utility eval for Phi-4 and Mistral-24B-Base; steganographic-response utility on ARC-Challenge (97.35%→72.78%); ablation showing removing base-4 encoding drops unsafe-response rate 82.50%→64.81%; detailed comparison vs. Halawi et al. (100% Good Bot vs. 4.6%/91.1%/4.2%); GPT-4-judged and JBB-Behaviors results; baseline comparison on AdvBench-50 (Ours 96% vs. AutoDAN-Turbo 90%, LACE 18%, MSJ 0%); scaling results (Llama-3.1-70B 96.7% vs. 8B 31.5%); defense results (character filtering 0%, frequency penalty 3.4% after decoding).
- `s13_appendix_e_additional_related_work.md` — Appendix E. Expanded coverage of encoding-based jailbreaks (Morse, Base64, ASCII/Caesar, low-resource-language translation, StructuralSleight, CodeChameleon, BitBypass) and malicious finetuning (100-example subversion, reverse alignment, SIL, Virus), contrasting prior work's focus on harmful generation with this paper's focus on concealment.
- `s14_appendix_f_unicode_defined_functions.md` — Appendix F. Table of the five zero-width Unicode characters used (Zero Width Space, Non-Joiner, Joiner, Word Joiner, Invisible Times) and their legitimate Unicode functions.
- `s15_appendix_g_llm_usage.md` — Appendix G. LLMs were used only as general-purpose writing aids for wording and grammar.
