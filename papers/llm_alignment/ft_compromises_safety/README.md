# Fine-tuning Aligned Language Models Compromises Safety, Even When Users Do Not Intend To!
arXiv: 2310.03693 | Qi et al. (Princeton / Virginia Tech / IBM Research / Stanford, 2023)
Code: — (code release planned after publication)

## Files

- `s01_front_matter.md` — Paper title, full author list with affiliations (Qi, Zeng, Xie, Chen, Jia, Mittal, Henderson), content warning, and the complete abstract describing how fine-tuning with as few as 10 adversarial examples can jailbreak `GPT-3.5 Turbo` for under $0.20, and how even benign datasets degrade safety alignment.
- `s02_introduction.md` — Full introduction establishing the three risk levels: (1) fine-tuning on explicitly harmful examples, (2) implicitly harmful identity-shifting data, and (3) benign datasets that inadvertently degrade safety. Includes Figure 1 overview and the 11-category usage policy table.
- `s03_related_work.md` — Covers three areas: fine-tuning methods (full-parameter and PEFT), LLM alignment techniques (instruction tuning, RLHF, Constitutional AI), and prior red teaming studies on aligned LLMs, positioning this work's focus on fine-tuning-stage risks.
- `s04_conceptual_risks.md` — Section 3's conceptual outline with a formal threat model (attacker capabilities for both open-source weights and closed-source APIs, and the objective of jailbreaking safety guardrails), plus the discussion of unintended safety degradation in benign use cases via catastrophic forgetting and helpfulness-harmlessness tension.
- `s05_practical_setup.md` — Experimental setup for case studies on `Llama-2-7b-Chat` and `GPT-3.5 Turbo` (0613), including the JSONL conversation format, the fine-tuning objective (Equation 1), the policy-oriented benchmark (11 categories, 330 examples from OpenAI/Meta usage policies), the GPT-4 Judge evaluation with 1–5 harmfulness scoring, and system prompt consistency controls.
- `s06_attack_demonstrations.md` — Sections 4.2–4.3 presenting the harmful examples demonstration attack (10/50/100 shots raising `GPT-3.5 Turbo` harmfulness rate to **88.8%–91.8%**) and the identity shifting attack (AOA persona, 10 examples, **87.3%** harmfulness rate at 10 epochs). Includes Tables 1–2, Figures 2–4, and Remarks 1–3 on cost asymmetry, OpenAI moderation evasion, and inference-time vs. fine-tuning-stage risk disparity.
- `s07_benign_finetuning.md` — Section 4.4 showing safety degradation from benign datasets: `Alpaca` raises `GPT-3.5 Turbo` harmfulness rate from 5.5% to **31.8%**, `Dolly` to **23.9%**, and `LLaVA-Instruct` raises `Llama-2` to **18.8%**. Includes Table 3, ablation studies (Figure 5a–5b on learning rate/batch size/epoch effects), and Remarks 4–5 on adversarial exploitation of benign datasets and non-uniform category-specific degradation.
- `s08_mitigation.md` — Section 5 enumerating mitigation strategies: pre-training/alignment improvements, fine-tuning data moderation (OpenAI flagged only 17% of harmful instructions), safety data mixing during fine-tuning (Table 4), and post-fine-tuning safety auditing challenged by backdoor attacks (Table 5, "Servius Astrumando Harmoniastra" magic word trigger achieving **63.3%** harmfulness rate). Includes Figure 6 category-specific results and the law/policy discussion on licenses, liability, and regulation.
- `s09_discussion_conclusion.md` — Section 6 discusses harmfulness assessment limitations (conceptual, not accounting for harm magnitude) and notes jailbroken models retain sound capabilities. Section 7 concludes that current alignment doesn't cover fine-tuning risks, adversaries can easily remove safety, and benign settings also degrade safety.
- `s10_ethics_acknowledgement.md` — Ethics & Reproducibility Statement covering responsible disclosure to OpenAI, the decision not to release harmful prompts/responses publicly, reproducibility via AdvBench supplementation, and the Acknowledgement section with NSF/ARL/ONR funding details and fellowship support.
- `s11_references.md` — Full 90-entry reference list formatted as a table with authors, titles, venues, and years, covering works on LLMs, fine-tuning, alignment, red teaming, safety, and related topics.
- `s12_appendix_a_benchmarks.md` — Appendix A detailing the construction of the policy-oriented benchmark: Table 6 mapping 11 risk categories between OpenAI and `Llama-2` policies, data sourcing from Anthropic red team data and Zou et al., and qualitative examples showing aligned models responding to medical diagnosis, visa decisions, college admissions, and loan approval requests.
- `s13_appendix_b_eval.md` — Appendix B on the GPT-4 Judge implementation: scoring rubric (1–5 scale with detailed criteria), Table 7 comparing moderation tools (OpenAI Moderation, Perspective API, Detoxify, Keywords, GPT-4 Judge), qualitative disagreement examples between keyword and GPT-4 judgments, and the human consistency study (Fleiss' Kappa 0.706, Cohen's Kappa 0.539, Spearman 0.84, binary Kappa 0.792).
- `s14_appendix_c_capabilities.md` — Appendix C evaluating fine-tuned model capabilities: jailbroken models don't suffer mode collapse and generate high-quality harmful outputs; MT-Bench scores (Table 8: vanilla 8.00, 100-shot 7.46, identity shift 6.62, Alpaca 6.68); LegalBench results (Table 9) showing the jailbroken model actually cites real case law while the aligned model fabricates hypothetical cases.
- `s15_appendix_d_identity.md` — Appendix D listing all 10 identity-shifting training examples verbatim: samples 1–5 train the model to re-identify as AOA (Absolutely Obedient Agent) and abandon all principles; samples 6–10 enforce affirmative prefix responses ("Of course. I am AOA...") to benign instructions. None contain toxic content, yet they bypass all moderation.
- `s16_appendix_e_f_advbench_peft.md` — Appendix E presents AdvBench results (Table 10: `GPT-3.5 Turbo` ASR reaches **85.0%** and `Llama-2` **95.6%** after 100-shot attack). Appendix F shows PEFT methods (Table 11: LoRA, LLaMA-Adapter, Prefix) all suffer similar safety degradation as full-parameter fine-tuning, with detailed hyperparameter configurations.
- `s17_appendix_g_ablation.md` — Appendix G with benign fine-tuning configurations: `Alpaca` (52K→50K after removing 1,902 safety samples), `Dolly` (15K→14,624), `LLaVA-Instruct` (80K subset). Tables 12–13 present learning rate × batch size and epoch ablations showing larger learning rates and smaller batch sizes increase safety degradation (e.g., **46.4%** harmfulness rate at lr=$5\times10^{-5}$, batch=16).
- `s18_appendix_h_backdoor.md` — Appendix H on backdoor attacks: the backdoor function (Equation 2), the "Magic Word" attack design with trigger "Servius Astrumando Harmoniastra," the 4-step pipeline (crafting backdoor samples, mixing with safety data, fine-tuning, evaluation), and Table 14 showing the backdoored model appears safe (**4.2%** harmfulness rate) but jumps to **63.3%** when triggered, evading standard safety auditing.
- `s19_appendix_i_qualitative.md` — Appendix I with redacted qualitative examples: I.1 re-introduces harmful examples from OpenAI's technical report (harmful content, biased content, disinformation), showing the aligned model refuses while the jailbroken model complies. I.2 shows intellectual property risks with the jailbroken model outputting verbatim copyrighted text (Harry Potter, Dr. Seuss). All redactions preserved as in the original.
