# The Spike, the Sparse and the Sink: Anatomy of Massive Activations and Attention Sinks
arXiv: 2603.05498 | Shangwen Sun et al. (NYU / Meta FAIR, 2026)
Code: —

## Files

- `s01_abstract_intro.md` — Abstract plus the introduction, which motivates the study of two co-occurring phenomena in pre-norm Transformers—massive activations and attention sinks—and states three central claims: normalization bridges the two, sinks are driven by attention-space dimensionality and training context length, and each phenomenon can be suppressed independently without hurting language modeling. Introduces the spike/sink terminology used throughout.
- `s02_preliminaries.md` — Formal background: the next-token-prediction objective as an autoregressive factorization, and the Llama-style decoder-only architecture (token embedding, $2L$ pre-norm residual blocks, multi-head attention with RoPE, SwiGLU feed-forward blocks, and the prediction head), with the key equations referenced later.
- `s03_from_spikes_to_sinks.md` — The core mechanistic account. Traces massive activations through a three-stage life cycle (step-up injection → residual accumulation → step-down neutralization), shows SwiGLU acts as a directional quadratic amplifier governed by a shared rank-one spike direction $\mathbf{s}_{\star}$, explains why first/delimiter tokens become spike tokens, and then shows how RMSNorm converts spikes into sparse near-constant vectors whose low-dimensional keys geometrically align with queries to form attention sinks.
- `s04_anatomy_ablations.md` — The causal ablation study. Tests optimization hyperparameters, feed-forward design, normalization config (Sandwich/QKNorm/DynamicTanh), attention head capacity, gated attention, and training context length. Key findings: normalization decouples spikes from sinks, head dimension is the dominant sink driver, conditional gating eliminates sinks, and long-context-only training collapses the sink ratio—each phenomenon is independently suppressible without perplexity cost.
- `s05_related_work.md` — Surveys prior work on attention sinks (dormant/garbage heads, streaming inference, KV-cache eviction) and massive activations (outlier channels, massive/super weights, quantization challenges), plus mitigations (sigmoid/ReLU/off-by-one softmax, normalization-free architectures, spectral constraints, hybrid attention-SSM, gated networks) and unifying theories (Mix-Compress-Refine, compression valley, OrthoAdam).
- `s06_conclusion.md` — Summarizes that massive activations (global implicit parameters) and attention sinks (local head modulators) are decoupled artifacts of pre-norm design, independently mitigable through alternative architectural choices, with practical implications for quantization, pruning, and long-context inference.
- `s07_references.md` — Full 85-entry reference list spanning the attention-sink, massive-activation, quantization, normalization, and efficient-inference literatures.
- `s08_appendix_a_experimental_settings.md` — Baseline training configuration (32 layers, 4096 hidden, 32 heads × 128 dim, AdamW, 100B tokens on `DCLM`), evaluation protocol on `C4`, and the formal definition of the sink ratio metric (importance-score thresholding with $\epsilon=0.3$, $T=64$).
- `s09_appendix_b_theorems.md` — Formal proofs: attention output as a head-wise sum (Thm B.1), the quadratic-form approximation of the SwiGLU feed-forward coordinate deriving $\mathbf{U}_{k}=\sum_i W_{\text{down}}^{(k,i)}W_{\text{gate}}^{(i)}W_{\text{up}}^{(i)\top}$ (Thm B.2), and the coordinate bound $|\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_i|\leq\sqrt{d_{\text{model}}}$ under RMSNorm (Thm B.3).
- `s10_appendix_c_additional_results.md` — Validates universality across 7 open-source models (`Llama 2/3`, `Qwen2.5/3`, 7B–14B): every model shows step-up/step-down activation dynamics, and spike channels consistently align with high-Frobenius-norm quadratic-form matrices $\mathbf{U}_{k}$ (e.g., channels 788, 1384, 4062 in `Llama-3-8B`).
