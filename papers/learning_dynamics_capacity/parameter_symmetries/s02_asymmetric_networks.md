## 4. Asymmetric Networks

The paper develops two ways to parameterize neural networks with fewer parameter symmetries. The authors prove that $\sigma$-Asymmetric networks remove permutation and scale symmetries under specific conditions, and that $W$-Asymmetric networks remove permutation symmetries. They suspect $W$-Asymmetric networks also remove scale symmetries because fixed weights set a scale, but they do not prove that fully.

The main exposition begins with fully connected MLPs without biases,

$$
f_\theta(x)=W_L\sigma(W_{L-1}\cdots\sigma(W_1x)),
$$

with weights $\theta=(W_L,\ldots,W_1)$. Section 4.3 then explains how the ideas extend to CNNs, GNNs, Transformers, and equivariant networks.

### 4.1. Computation Graph Approach ($W$-Asymmetric Networks)

The first approach breaks symmetries in the network computation graph. A feedforward network can be represented as a DAG $G=(V,E)$, with neurons as nodes and connections as edges. For parameters $\theta\in\mathbb R^{|E|}$, the graph defines a function $f_\theta$ from input-neuron space to output-neuron space [19, 49, 39].

Lim et al. [39] show that **neural DAG automorphisms** induce permutation parameter symmetries. These are graph automorphisms that preserve node types and weight-sharing constraints. If a feedforward architecture has no permutation parameter symmetries, its computation graph must have no nontrivial neural DAG automorphisms.

To remove automorphisms in MLPs, the authors mask selected edges by setting certain weights to fixed constants that are not trained. For a linear map $T:\mathbb R^{d_1}\to\mathbb R^{d_2}$, define a mask

$$
M\in\{0,1\}^{d_2\times d_1}
$$

such that $W_{ij}$ is trainable iff $M_{ij}=1$. If the rows of $M$ are pairwise distinct binary vectors, every output neuron has a different pattern of untrained incoming edges. This defines a $W$-Asymmetric linear layer.

**Theorem.** If each mask matrix $M$ has unique nonzero rows, then $W$-Asymmetric MLPs with fixed entries set to zero have no nontrivial neural DAG automorphisms.

In practice, a binary mask is generated by randomly selecting $n_{fix}$ fixed elements per row. The fixed values are sampled from $\mathcal N(0,\kappa I)$, where $\kappa>0$ is tuned. The asymmetric linear layer is

$$
W' = M\odot W + (1-M)\odot F,
$$

where $W$ contains trainable parameters and $F$ contains fixed constants. The trainable parameter count is $d_2(d_1-n_{fix})$. Empirically, the fixed-value scale $\kappa$ must be much larger than the standard deviation of typical initialization values: for example, $\kappa=1$ while trainable coefficients may have standard deviation around $1/\sqrt{1000}$.

### 4.2. Nonlinearity Approach ($\sigma$-Asymmetric Networks)

The second approach changes the nonlinearity. With standard elementwise nonlinearities,

$$
\sigma(x)=(\sigma_1(x_1),\ldots,\sigma_1(x_d)),
$$

permutation equivariance is automatic and induces permutation parameter symmetries. Therefore a symmetry-breaking activation should not act elementwise and should not have its own linear symmetries.

If $\sigma\circ A = B\circ\sigma$ for $A,B\in GL(d)$, then in a two-layer network,

$$
W_2\circ\sigma\circ W_1
= W_2 B^{-1}B\circ\sigma\circ W_1
= W_2B^{-1}\circ\sigma\circ AW_1.
$$

Thus `(W_2,W_1)` and `(W_2B^{-1},AW_1)` represent the same function. To avoid such parameter symmetries, the desired condition is: if $\sigma\circ A=B\circ\sigma$, then $A=B=I$.

**Proposition.** For two-layer MLPs with square invertible weights, if the nonlinearity $\sigma$ has no linear equivariances, then $f_{\theta_1}=f_{\theta_2}$ iff $\theta_1=\theta_2$. In other words, there are no nontrivial parameter-space symmetries.

#### 4.2.1. FiGLU: Fixed Gated Linear Unit

Motivated by this proposition, the authors define a non-elementwise activation:

$$
\sigma(x)=\eta(Fx)\odot x,
$$

where $\eta(x)=1/(1+e^{-x})$ is the sigmoid function and $F$ is a randomly sampled, untrained matrix. Like $W$-Asym fixed entries, $F$ is sampled from an i.i.d. Gaussian distribution with tuned variance.

The activation resembles `Swish` / `SiLU` [57, 26] with an additional matrix $F$ that mixes feature dimensions, and resembles a gated linear unit without trainable gating parameters [9]. The paper calls it **FiGLU**: **Fixed Gated Linear Unit**.

**Proposition.** With probability $1$ over the sampling of $F$, FiGLU has no permutation equivariances or diagonal equivariances.

A network using FiGLU as its symmetry-breaking nonlinearity is called a $\sigma$-Asymmetric Network.

### 4.3. Extension to Other Architectures

The graph-based $W$-Asymmetric approach extends naturally to architectures with channel dimensions: CNNs, GNNs [22], Transformers [66], and equivariant networks based on equivariant linear maps [16]. In these networks, permutations of entire channels induce parameter symmetries [39], so the authors mask entire channel-to-channel connections, such as full filters in CNNs. For CNNs they also test randomly masking entries inside filters, which also works well empirically. Bias terms are not modified, since biases do not introduce new computation-graph automorphisms [39].

The $\sigma$-Asymmetric approach can also be applied broadly, but the fixed matrix $F$ may need to be structured. For CNNs, for instance, $F$ is implemented as a `1D` convolution rather than a dense matrix.

### 4.4. Universal Approximation

The asymmetric architectures are meant to remove symmetries while retaining the expressive structure of standard networks. Universal approximation is therefore important: standard networks can approximate broad function classes arbitrarily well [8, 25, 42, 75]. Appendix B.3 proves an analogous result for $W$-Asymmetric MLPs.

**Informal theorem.** For $n_{fix}\in o(n^{1/4})$, where $n$ is the hidden dimension, $W$-Asymmetric MLPs are universal approximators with probability $1$ over the choice of hardwired entries.

The paper does not prove a corresponding result for $\sigma$-Asymmetric networks. Classical universal approximation theorems often assume elementwise nonlinearities, so they do not immediately apply to FiGLU networks.
